Company Overview

What They Do

Maxgrow India Limited (BSE: 521167, NSE: MAXGROW) presents itself as a ferrous and non-ferrous metals trading company, with company positioning language referencing "industrial solutions," "sustainable recycling" and "value-added processing" of scrap metal. No independently-verified segment data, plant capacity, or processing-volume disclosure was found anywhere in this research to substantiate the "recycling/processing" framing beyond the company's own marketing language.

The disclosed financial pattern is more consistent with a different business than the one implied by that language: MarketSmith's standalone-basis summary shows near-zero standalone operating revenue (Section 17), consolidated operating margins have run at approximately 1.8% across all four disclosed quarters (Section 08) — consistent with razor-thin commodity pass-through trading spreads, not value-added manufacturing — and debtor days stand at a very high 278 days (Section 08), a working-capital pattern typical of a trading intermediary rather than a processor with owned inventory cycles. Readers should treat "recycling" and "processing" as unverified positioning claims, not confirmed operating facts.

Corporate History — Essential Context, Not a Footnote: The entity now trading as Maxgrow India has existed under four different business identities since 1994: Apeego (1994, garment exporter) → Frontline Business Solutions (2008, BPO/HR-services company) → Inanna Fashion and Trends (2016, fashion retailer) → renamed Maxgrow India in 2018, with the metals-trading business itself only commencing following the NCLT-approved insolvency-resolution (CIRP) process concluded Dec 2023 (Section 02). The current "metals trading" identity is therefore only ~2-3 years old — not a long-established core competency, and not the same operating business that generates its listed track record.

What Happened & Why It Matters Beyond the Headline

Maxgrow India's entire current existence traces back to a court-supervised corporate rebirth. NCLT Mumbai admitted the insolvency case on 6 Jun 2021; the Committee of Creditors approved PP Metallix Limited's resolution plan on 24 Jan 2022; and NCLT gave final approval on 6 Dec 2023. The board was reconstituted on 10 Dec 2024, and via the resolution-plan share allotment mechanism — not a conventional QIP or rights issue — promoter holding jumped from 16.22% (Sep 2024) to 92.82% (Dec 2024/Mar 2025) in a single quarter (Section 06).

Revenue then scaled at a pace with essentially no precedent in this company's history: from a near-dormant base to ₹2,758 Cr in Q4FY25, and further to ₹6,106 Cr by Q3FY26 (Section 08). This is a genuine, dateable, verifiable operational restart under new ownership — not a gradual turnaround of an existing operating business.

Caveat — timing pattern worth flagging, not accusing: the revenue explosion coincides very tightly with the promoter-stake jump to 92.82%, while public free float collapsed to ~7% over the same period. This report does not allege wrongdoing — no specific finding against Maxgrow India was located in this research — but the tight coincidence of ownership change, float collapse, and revenue explosion is a pattern worth the reader's own scrutiny, not something to wave past.
Framing note: Maxgrow India is a recently-reconstituted entity with roughly a 2-year operating track record under its current ownership and business model — not a mature company with a multi-year history of the business it is now valued on. Every metric in this report that appears to show "growth" should be read against that short base, not as an established trend line.

Sector Market Size

Estimates for India's scrap metal recycling market vary meaningfully across sources (~$12-13bn in 2026, growing at ~7.2-8.4% CAGR to potentially ~$22bn by 2031), shown as range midpoints rather than a single fabricated figure. India's steel scrap consumption is estimated at ~45 million tonnes in FY2026, against a policy backdrop set by the Steel Scrap Recycling Policy 2019 (47 Electric Arc Furnaces and 1,128 Induction Furnaces relying on scrap as of March 2019) and the National Steel Policy 2017 target of 300 MTPA capacity by 2030, with 35-40% via the EAF/IF route. India remains a large net scrap importer — ~8.04 million tonnes in 2025, the world's #2 importer — sourcing chiefly from the UAE, UK, USA and Saudi Arabia. Nil Basic Customs Duty on ferrous scrap imports continues only through 31 Mar 2026 (a time-bound exemption, not permanent), while non-ferrous scrap retains a 2.5% BCD.
Genuine policy headwind, not a one-directional tailwind: a 27 Mar 2026 draft amendment removed "other steel scrap materials" from EPR-certificate eligibility under the Vehicle Scrappage / End-of-Life Vehicle Rules, and India's auto sector missed its FY26 ELV scrappage target by ~70%. This is a real headwind to the feedstock-supply thesis for scrap traders, presented here for balance rather than folded silently into the growth narrative above.
