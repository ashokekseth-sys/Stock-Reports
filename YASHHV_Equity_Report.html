Company Overview

What They Do
Classification correction, stated once, plainly: Yash Highvoltage is not a general power-line/disc/pin insulator manufacturer. Several early research angles on this name defaulted to that broader, more commodity classification before it was corrected here. The company is a specialist maker of high-voltage transformer bushings — the insulated component that carries a conductor safely through a transformer's tank wall — a narrower, more technical niche than commodity line/disc insulators. This correction is independently corroborated by MarketSmith's own written description of the company (Section 17). All framing in this report uses the corrected, bushings-specific description from this point forward.

Yash Highvoltage manufactures Oil Impregnated Paper (OIP) condenser bushings, Resin Impregnated Paper (RIP) and Resin Impregnated Synthetic (RIS) condenser bushings, High Current Bushings, and oil-to-oil bushings, across voltage classes of 24-170kV, with an EHV upgrade to 550kV underway (Section 02). It also makes fibreglass cylinders and FRP guard rings as insulation input materials used in its own and third-party bushing manufacture. Products serve transformer OEMs and utilities globally, with a stated retrofit/customization focus aimed at extending in-service transformer life.

The company was incorporated as a private company on 6 June 2002 in Gujarat and converted to a public company on 19 February 2018. It began manufacturing RIP condenser bushings under a technology license from Moser-Glaser (MGC), Switzerland — an RIP pioneer since the 1960s — from 2016, a genuine and credible technology partnership, though it should be read precisely as licensed, not owned, IP (Section 03). The company listed on the BSE SME platform on 19 December 2024 (IPO band ₹138-146/share, ₹110 Cr raised, ~170-182x subscribed across sources, +89.7% listing-day gain), and now trades under both BSE 544310 and NSE YASHHV. The exact date of its SME-to-mainboard migration could not be confirmed from sources reviewed this cycle — flagged plainly rather than assumed; a reader wanting the precise migration date should verify directly against BSE/NSE circulars.

The company states it serves ~1,000 customers across 50+ countries. Certifications include ISO 9001:2015, IEC-60137:2017 and IEEE C57.19.00/01, backed by an in-house 505kV high-voltage test facility and a 1,500kVp impulse test facility equipped with Omicron partial-discharge test equipment — the most defensible, verifiable evidence of technical credibility in this profile (Section 03).

Capacity Profile — No Product-Mix Breakdown Disclosed
Yash Highvoltage discloses no percentage-based product-mix or segment breakdown across its bushing types (OIP/RIP/RIS/High Current) — it is reported and analyzed here as a single-segment bushings business. Rather than fabricate a product-mix split, this space is used for the one genuinely disclosed capacity figure: current manufacturing capacity of ~7,000 bushings/year, expanding toward ~15,000-16,000 units/year as the new Vadodara greenfield facility (Section 02) comes online.
Segment disclosure gap: No India-specific or product-line-specific revenue split (OIP vs. RIP vs. RIS vs. High Current Bushings) was found in sources reviewed this cycle. A reader wanting that granularity should check the company's latest annual report segment note directly.

What Happened & Why It Matters

Three related developments define the current period. FY26 results confirmed the growth story: revenue rose +57% YoY to ₹235.2 Cr, PAT rose +74% YoY to ₹37.3 Cr, EBITDA rose +75% YoY to ₹60.4 Cr at a 25.7% margin, and the order book more than doubled — up +166% YoY to ₹400+ Cr. This is a company reinvesting aggressively in capacity and technology class ahead of a genuine sector tailwind (Section 05), not a one-quarter blip.

To fund that reinvestment, the board approved a ₹151 Cr preferential issue on 23 June 2026, shareholders approved it at an EGM on 15 July 2026, and allotment completed on 6-7 August 2026 — comprising 12.62 lakh equity shares plus 8.32 lakh convertible warrants at ₹721/share. Proceeds are earmarked for the new Vadodara greenfield facility: backward integration into RIP/RIS core manufacturing (currently imported inputs being brought in-house), plus an EHV upgrade from 245kV to 550kV bushing capability. Trial production is targeted for Q2 FY27, with commercial output targeted for H2 FY27, adding ~5,000-6,000 units/year toward a combined ~15,000-16,000 unit/year total capacity (Section 01).

Caveat — the preferential issue is dilutive, flagged plainly: promoter holding fell from 57.94% (Mar 2026) to 54.79% (Aug 2026) — a 3.15 percentage-point decline in a single quarter — directly as a result of this capital raise (Section 06), not open-market selling. Separately, three early non-promoter investors — Negen Undiscovered Value Fund, YR Investment Opportunities Fund, and Neil Madan Bahal — sold a combined 2.27% stake via the open market between August 2025 and June 2026, trimming their combined holding from 5.73% to 3.46%. Neither data point is alarming on its own, but both are relevant shareholding-quality items during a period of extraordinary share-price appreciation (Section 04, 07).

Near-term watch items: (1) whether the Vadodara ramp executes on the stated H2 FY27 commercial-production timeline; and (2) whether the working-capital strain discussed in Section 08-09 — a 187-day cash conversion cycle and negative free cash flow despite strong reported profitability — eases as the new capacity comes online.
Update — disclosed cyber-fraud incident: the company disclosed a ₹2.10 Cr Business Email Compromise (BEC) fraud — ₹1.36 Cr paid to a fraudulent party for goods that were actually received, and ₹0.74 Cr paid for goods that were never delivered. Complaints were filed with the Vadodara Police and the National Cyber Crime Reporting Portal on 9 April 2026; the company states operations were unaffected and it is pursuing recovery through its bankers. This reads as resolved in terms of operational continuity, but a genuinely material internal-controls incident worth the reader's attention rather than a footnote — see the management-quality checklist in Section 11.

Synthesis: The overall moat is best read as MODERATE, concentrated almost entirely in certification/qualification barriers and licensed process know-how — not in cost, brand, or owned IP. The company positions itself as "the world's only independent, specialist" bushing manufacturer, citing 45,000+ installations and use in PGCIL STATCOM projects — a credible niche reputation (Brand: moderate), but a small player globally against branded giants like Hitachi Energy, so brand alone is not evidence of durable pricing power. The most genuine structural advantage is switching costs: bushings are type-tested to IEC-60137/IEEE C57.19 at accredited labs, and utility/transformer-OEM vendor-qualification cycles are genuinely long — real stickiness once qualified, though it does not prevent dual-sourcing on new tenders. Cost advantage is weak: there is no evidence of raw-material or energy cost advantage, and the company itself cites exposure to global epoxy resin and porcelain price volatility as a margin risk, not an advantage (Section 15), against a modest ~7,000-bushing/year scale versus global majors. Patents/proprietary tech is moderate: RIP condenser bushing manufacturing under a technology license from Moser-Glaser (MGC), Switzerland since 2016 is genuine and credible, but it is licensed, not owned, IP — no evidence was found of company-held patents specifically. This is a real but narrow moat appropriate to a small, growing specialist, not a dominant, structurally-protected incumbent.

Sector Market Size

India's power transmission equipment market is estimated at ~$12.4bn (2025), growing to ~$22.2bn by 2034 (~6.53% CAGR). The broader T&D EPC market is estimated at ~$14.68bn (2025) growing to ~$35.2bn by 2035 (~9.34% CAGR). India's insulators market specifically — a broader category than transformer bushings, cited here for sector context — is estimated at ~$696mn-750mn+ by 2032 (6.4-7.8% CAGR range, per this report series' convention for wide analyst estimates). The core demand driver is the need to evacuate ~500GW of renewable capacity by 2030 (~600GW by 2032), which requires materially more transformation and transmission infrastructure — and every transformer needs bushings.
Policy tailwinds: the National Electricity Plan (Transmission) targets ~₹9 lakh crore (~$109bn) of investment, 191,000+ ckm of new transmission lines, and 1,270 GVA of transformation capacity by 2032, with inter-regional transfer capacity rising from 119GW today to 143GW (2027) to 168GW (2032). The Green Energy Corridor Phases 3 & 4 target evacuation of 150GW of renewable capacity, and PM Gati Shakti's ₹100 lakh crore multimodal infrastructure plan includes power/energy transmission as an integrated layer.
Real, current headwind — not a one-directional tailwind: cheap Chinese imports are cited industry-wide as eroding margins for insulator/bushing-adjacent manufacturers — a genuine competitive pressure layered on top of the growth story, not a hypothetical risk.
