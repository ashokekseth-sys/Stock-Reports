Company Overview

What They Do

Vesuvius India Limited manufactures and trades refractory products for the steel and foundry industries — principally flow control refractories (slide-gate systems, stopper rods, tundish refractories, ladle shrouds, and isostatically-pressed consumables used in continuous casting), along with advanced/basic refractories, digital measurement refractory systems, and crucible refractories. These are consumable, safety-critical products fitted at the point where molten steel is handled — ladles, tundishes and casters — not commodity building materials.

The company was incorporated in September 1991 as Vesuvius Refractories Limited, renamed Vesuvius India Limited in November 1992, listed via IPO in September 1993, and began commercial production at its first factory in Kolkata on 1 July 1994. It also operates a monolithics plant at Visakhapatnam (acquired from Carborundum Universal), and has historically licensed Blast Furnace Cast technology from Answer Technologies Inc. (USA). The company is a 55.57%-owned subsidiary of UK-listed Vesuvius plc, a global leader in molten-metal flow engineering, with that stake stable since at least 2017 (Section 06).

Listing history — precise, not a footnote: Vesuvius India voluntarily delisted from active trading on the BSE in May 2016. BSE code 520113 continues to appear in reference data and is retained on the cover of this report for identification purposes, but NSE (VESUVIUS) is the company's primary and effectively only active listing today. This is a routine exchange-consolidation move by an already-NSE-listed company, not a going-private or squeeze-out event — flagged here precisely, per Section 15, rather than left ambiguous.

Vesuvius India carries superior margins and returns on capital to both listed peers shown in Section 14 (17% OPM, 15% ROE, 21.3% ROCE), but has been steadily losing market share (~3% over six years) to RHI Magnesita India, which is larger, more vertically integrated (captive raw-material mines, ~85% utilization vs. an industry average of 50-60%), and holds outright share leadership. What Happened & Why It Matters

Four related developments define the current period rather than one single headline deal. Mohinder Pradip Singh Rajput was appointed Additional Director/Managing Director effective 1 Jul 2024 for a 5-year term, ratified by postal ballot on 2 Aug 2024; he previously led strategic growth and regional CEO roles at Lhoist and ran ERAMET's Nickel and then Manganese divisions. He replaces Nitin Jain, who moved to a global Vesuvius Group role in the UK and remains on the VIL board as a Non-Executive Non-Independent Director — an orderly succession pattern, not a disruptive exit.

The company also executed its first-ever 10:1 stock split (face value ₹10 → ₹1, record date 10 Jun 2025), following a ₹14.50/share final dividend recommended on 26 Feb 2025 (pre-split basis). In parallel, it is deploying a ~₹1,000 Cr cumulative capex program (~₹650-700 Cr already spent) building three new greenfield plants at the Visakhapatnam Industrial Complex — a Mould Flux plant, an Alumino-Silicate Monolithics plant (from May 2025) and a Basic Monolithics plant (from June 2025) — adding ~250,000 tonnes/year of capacity, plus a Kolkata brownfield "VISO" expansion (12-18 month timeline). This reads as a company actively reinvesting in Indian capacity ahead of the steel-sector capex supercycle (Section 05), under a newly installed leadership team.

Most recently, the board approved (Aug 2026) a slump sale of the Mehsana, Gujarat facility — which makes crucibles, stoppers and sleeves for the non-ferrous foundry segment — to Foseco India Limited for ₹43.25 Cr, targeted to close by 31 Dec 2026, as part of a group-wide non-ferrous foundry portfolio realignment.

Caveat — the Mehsana sale is an intercompany transaction, flagged plainly: Foseco India is a distinct, separately-listed sister company also controlled by Vesuvius plc (which owns 64% of it) — not the same entity as Vesuvius India, and this report does not conflate the two. While the sale is presented by the company as portfolio rationalization, it is being struck between two entities under common UK-parent control, and this report cannot independently verify that the ₹43.25 Cr price was set on arm's-length terms — royalty and related-party-transaction quantum disclosure was not accessible this cycle (Section 10). This is not an allegation of impropriety; it is a genuine verification gap the reader should close via the transaction's SEBI/BSE disclosures and the annual report's Related Party Transactions note.
Near-term watch items: (1) execution of the capacity ramp at Visakhapatnam and whether the new plants deliver the intended volume/margin benefit; and (2) whether the MD transition changes India capital-allocation priorities relative to the prior leadership's approach — both are genuinely open questions rather than settled outcomes at this point.

Synthesis: The overall moat is best read as MODERATE. Vesuvius plc's global reputation lends real credibility/qualification value with steelmakers (Brand: moderate), but has not translated into pricing power sufficient to prevent share loss. The most genuine structural advantage is switching costs: refractory failure causes costly, unplanned furnace shutdowns, so steel mills undertake extensive supplier qualification and often prefer "Total Refractory Management" relationships — a real barrier, though one that protects incumbency in general rather than guaranteeing any single supplier's position. Technology/know-how transfer from the Vesuvius plc group's global flow-control R&D is genuine (Patents: moderate), but industry analysis suggests "special/high-value refractories constitute only a minimal portion of Vesuvius India's sales" — meaning most of the India business competes in more commoditized, replicable categories where that edge matters less. Most importantly, Vesuvius India has no disclosed captive raw-material integration in India and is import-dependent for magnesite, graphite and other inputs (Cost Advantage: weak) — versus RHI Magnesita India, which sources ~40% from captive mines across Brazil, Turkey and Europe and runs at ~85% utilization against a 50-60% industry average. This moat helps Vesuvius India compete profitably, but has not been enough to let it win share from a bigger, more integrated rival (Section 14).

Sector Market Size

India's refractories market is estimated at ~$2.6-2.8bn (FY2024/25), growing at ~7.4-8.3% CAGR through 2030-32 (range midpoints shown, per this report series' convention for wide analyst estimates). Iron & steel is the largest end-use segment. India's steel capacity reached ~222 MTPA as of June 2026, on track toward the National Steel Policy's 300 MTPA-by-2030 target (250 MT actual production goal, ~160kg per-capita consumption by 2030-31, backed by an estimated ₹10 lakh crore of investment). Refractories line every furnace, ladle, and tundish, so this is a direct multi-year volume driver rather than an indirect one. India's foundry/casting market adds a further ~$22-29bn (2025/26), growing ~11-15.6% CAGR through 2030-2034, on vehicle production, machinery and exports. The global refractories market is cited at ~$50.9bn (2026) across sources reviewed.
Green-steel/EAF transition — a tailwind here, not a headwind: unlike ICE-to-EV transitions elsewhere in this report series, the shift toward Electric Arc Furnace and lower-carbon steelmaking is reformulation demand for refractories — new thermal-shock- and hydrogen-resistant linings are needed as furnace chemistry changes — not a demand threat. This is cited here as a growth driver.
